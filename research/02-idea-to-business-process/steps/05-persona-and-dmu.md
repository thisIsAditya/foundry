# Step 05 — Persona and decision-making unit

**Phase:** 1  
**Sources:** Aulet steps 5 and 12; Value Proposition Canvas; B2B MEDDIC (economic buyer).

## What this step is

Turn the beachhead into **one persona** (a realistic person, ideally based on a real interviewee) and map everyone who can say yes or no to a purchase.

### Persona

Name, role, day-in-the-life, tools, fears, and **ranked purchase criteria** (Aulet: the top two become the competitive chart axes in step 07). Example: (1) “must work with Tally,” (2) “CA will not yell,” (3) price.

### Decision-making unit (DMU)

| Role | Question |
|---|---|
| End user | Who touches it daily? |
| Champion | Who wants this to happen? |
| Economic buyer | Who can spend the money? |
| Influencers | Whose opinion moves the buyer? |
| Veto | IT, legal, spouse, CA, franchise HQ? |
| Purchasing / procurement | Is there a formal vendor process? |

In Indian SMEs the economic buyer is often the owner; the veto is often the CA or the son who “does computers.” In households it may be a spouse. In schools it may be a trust. **Wrong DMU = you built for a user who cannot buy.**

## Evidence required

- Persona grounded in interviews, not a stock photo template.  
- At least one interview that answered “how would this get approved?”  
- Budget band: petty cash vs owner WhatsApp vs formal PO.

## Common failure modes

- Five personas on day 0.  
- Ignoring the CA / IT / parent.  
- Consumer persona for a product the company must buy.
