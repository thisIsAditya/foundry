# Customer Development (Steve Blank)

**Source:** *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (2005; Stanford course text widely used). Blank later co-created Lean LaunchPad / I-Corps.

## The premise

Startups fail because they execute a **product development** process (spec → build → test → launch → position) as if customers were known. They are not. Customer Development is a **sibling process** that runs in parallel, with its own milestones. Extra cash in the first two steps is often harmful: it lets you discount, give the product away, and hide the fact that nobody will pay.

Blank’s line: **there are no facts inside the building, so get outside.**

## The four steps

### 1. Customer Discovery

Founders (not a junior “market research intern”) turn guesses into written hypotheses: who the customer is, what problem they have, whether it is important, what the solution might be. Then they test those hypotheses in person. The goal is to understand **problems and needs**, not to pitch.

Deliverable: a proven problem, a proposed solution hypothesis, and a first sketch of the business model — or a pivot.

### 2. Customer Validation

Prove there is a **repeatable, scalable sales process**. The founders sell. You are not hiring a VP of Sales to figure this out. If you cannot get orders (or equivalent commitment) with a known motion, you return to Discovery. This is the pivot loop.

Deliverable: a sales roadmap that someone else could later execute.

### 3. Customer Creation

Only now do you spend to **create end-user demand**. Marketing programs depend on market type (new market vs existing market vs clone). Blank’s point: demand-gen before validation is how you light money on fire.

### 4. Company Building

The org that was designed for **search** (flexible, learning, founders in every sale) becomes an org designed for **execution** (departments, process, managers). Doing this too early is “playing house.”

## Market type (often skipped, still useful)

Blank insists you know whether you are:

- Entering an **existing** market (compare on features/price; faster sales cycle; bloody competition),
- Creating a **new** market (educate; long sales; no comparables),
- **Re-segmenting** (low-cost or niche),
- Or **cloning** a model into a new geography.

This changes GTM, not just the slide title.

## What to encode in agents

- Hypothesis objects, not essays.
- Founder must be in the interview path (the Interview Coach can prepare, not replace).
- Validation = money or equivalent, plus a documented sales motion.
- Block “growth agent” spend until Validation artifacts exist.
- Company-building agents (HR, process, OKRs) stay locked until Gate C.

## What not to encode blindly

- Silicon Valley “get out of the building” can mean Zoom/WhatsApp in India. The rule is **talk to strangers who have the problem**, not “fly to a cafe.”
- Blank is light on unit economics formulas; pair with Aulet steps 17–19.
