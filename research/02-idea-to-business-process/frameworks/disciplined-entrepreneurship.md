# Disciplined Entrepreneurship (Bill Aulet, MIT)

**Source:** *Disciplined Entrepreneurship: 24 Steps to a Successful Startup* (2013; expanded 2nd ed. 2024). Aulet is Managing Director of the Martin Trust Center for MIT Entrepreneurship.

## The premise

Entrepreneurship can be taught as **discrete behaviors**. The method is for **Innovation-Driven Enterprises** (IDEs), not small shops that copy a known model — though many steps still help a cafe or agency. Famous line: **the single necessary and sufficient condition for a startup to succeed is a paying customer.**

The 24 steps are **integrated and iterative**, not a waterfall. Later evidence rewrites earlier steps.

## The 24 steps (canonical list)

**Who is your customer?**

1. Market segmentation  
2. Select a beachhead market  
3. Build an end-user profile  
4. Calculate TAM for the beachhead (bottom-up; 100% share revenue)  
5. Profile the persona  

**What can you do for them?**

6. Full life-cycle use case  
7. High-level product specification (visual, cheap, not a build)  
8. Quantified value proposition (as-is vs to-be, numbers)  
9. Identify your next 10 customers  
10. Define your core (defensible “secret sauce”)  
11. Chart competitive position on the persona’s top two priorities  

**How do they acquire it?**

12. Decision-making unit (champion, end user, economic buyer, vetoes)  
13. Map the process to acquire a paying customer  

**How do you make money?**

14. TAM for follow-on markets (bowling-pin expansion; do not chase them yet)  
15. Design a business model (17 example patterns; “free” is not a model)  
16. Pricing framework (value-based; easier to start high)  
17. LTV of an acquired customer (often 5-year, discounted, gross margin)  
18. Map sales process short/medium/long term  
19. COCA / CAC by phase  

**How do you design and build?**

20. Identify key assumptions  
21. Test key assumptions cheaply  
22. Define the **Minimum Viable Business Product** (value + payment + feedback loop)  
23. Show that “the dogs will eat the dog food”  

**How do you scale?**

24. Develop a product plan (complete the beachhead product; then follow-ons)

## Numbers Aulet actually uses (encode as guidance, not law)

- Beachhead TAM often healthiest in **~$20–100 million / year**. Under ~$5M may not sustain a venture-scale company (a lifestyle business can still be fine — say so). A $1B “beachhead” usually means the segment is not a beachhead.
- LTV **≥ ~3×** COCA as a target, with COCA falling over time.
- Discount rates for LTV in startups can be very high (he discusses 30–75% — this is teaching caution, not a finance-department WACC).

## MVBP vs MVP

Three requirements:

1. Customer gets the core value.  
2. Customer **pays**.  
3. The artifact is enough to start a feedback loop.

Two-sided markets must satisfy both sides (users may be free; the paying side still pays).

## What to encode

- Beachhead + persona + DMU as required objects.  
- Quantified value proposition as a first-class card.  
- Bottom-up TAM with method notes.  
- Competitive chart on **customer priorities**, not on a random 2×2.  
- MVBP definition in the scoper agent.  
- Follow-on TAM as a *story*, with a warning not to execute it yet.

## What not to encode

- Forcing every kirana or freelance idea through a $20M TAM filter.  
- Building the full 24-step binder before a single interview (Aulet still sends you to talk in Step 1).  
- Treating “core” as “we use AI.”
