# The Mom Test and how to take discovery interviews

**Source:** Rob Fitzpatrick, _The Mom Test_ (2013). The title: even your mom will lie if you ask “would you use my app?” Ask about **their life**, and even she will tell the truth.

## The three rules

1. Talk about **their life**, not your idea.
2. Ask about **specifics in the past**, not generics in the future.
3. Talk less. Listen more.

Compliments, fluff, and hypotheticals are worthless. “That’s so cool, I would definitely buy that” is a failed interview.

## What good questions look like

- “Last time this happened, what did you do?”
- “How much time/money did that cost?”
- “Who else was involved in the decision?”
- “What have you already tried? What did you pay for it?”
- “If you could wave a wand, what would change tomorrow?” (use sparingly; still hypothetical)

## What bad questions look like

- “Would you use an app that…?”
- “How much would you pay for…?” (ask what they **already pay**, then later run a real WTP test)
- Pitching for 20 minutes and asking “so, interested?”

## Practical design (also I-Corps / Blank)

- Founders do the interviews. Agents **prepare scripts, take notes, extract quotes, flag Mom Test violations**.
- Record with consent (DPDP: this is personal data).
- Stop pitching. The Blutrich interview-agent encodes this as a cardinal rule — keep it.
- Segment: a few people in 2–3 candidate segments beats 20 people in “SMEs.”
- End by asking for **introductions** to others with the same problem (Aulet’s next-10).

## Counts

There is no magic 15. The starter notes’ 15–20 is a reasonable **minimum for a focused beachhead**, not a scientific sample. Stop when you hear the same pain, workaround, and budget story on repeat — or when you cannot find anyone who has the problem.

## Encoding for agents

- Live violation detector: “you just pitched,” “hypothetical future,” “would you.”
- Quote extraction with timestamp.
- Pain score: frequency × severity × current spend.
- A meeting that produces only compliments fails the gate.
