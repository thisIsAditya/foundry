# Frameworks compared

Use this as the “why our steps look like this” table. None of these frameworks is a complete company OS. We compose them.

## At a glance

| Framework                    | Author                 | Core unit                                  | Starts with                   | Ends with                              | Weakness                                |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Customer Development         | Steve Blank            | Four steps parallel to product development | Hypotheses + “get outside”    | Company building (execution org)       | Light on TAM math and India law         |
| Lean Startup                 | Eric Ries              | Build–Measure–Learn                        | Leap-of-faith assumptions     | Validated learning, pivot or persevere | Easy to ship junk and call it an MVP    |
| Disciplined Entrepreneurship | Bill Aulet (MIT)       | 24 sequential-but-iterative steps          | Market segmentation           | Product plan after paid use            | Heavy; can delay shipping               |
| Business Model Canvas        | Osterwalder            | 9 blocks of an existing/known model        | Infrastructure + offer        | How the firm creates/captures value    | Too “company-shaped” for day-0          |
| Lean Canvas                  | Ash Maurya             | 9 blocks of uncertainty                    | Problem, segments             | Unfair advantage (often blank)         | Static if not tied to experiments       |
| Value Proposition Canvas     | Osterwalder            | Jobs / pains / gains vs product            | Customer profile              | Fit                                    | Not a business model                    |
| The Mom Test                 | Rob Fitzpatrick        | Interview quality                          | Their past behavior           | Truth instead of compliments           | Not a full process                      |
| YC / Sequoia practice        | Practitioners          | Traction and narrative                     | Users and a wedge             | Seed story or scale                    | US-centric legal/fundraising            |
| Pretotyping                  | Alberto Savoia         | Test the idea before the product           | Fake door, landing, concierge | “Would they use it?”                   | Not “would they pay?” unless you charge |
| JTBD                         | Christensen et al.     | Job the customer hires a product to do     | Context of struggle           | Better job performance                 | Easy to get academic                    |
| Traction                     | Weinberg / Mares       | 19 channels, test in parallel small        | Bullseye                      | A working channel                      | Can become spray-and-pray ads           |
| PMF tests                    | Sean Ellis, Superhuman | Survey / return-to-use                     | Existing users                | % who would be “very disappointed”     | Useless before you have users           |

## How they nest

```
Blank:  Discovery ──────── Validation ──── Creation ──── Company building
Ries:   hypotheses → experiments → learn  (throughout Discovery+Validation)
Aulet:  who is customer → what you do for them → how they buy → how you make money
        → how you build → how you scale
Maurya: canvas of guesses → riskiest assumption → 2-week tests
Fitzpatrick: the quality bar on every conversation in the left half
India law: sits under “can we take money / data / employees” from Validation onward
```

## Six questions (Aulet’s 2024 grouping) mapped to our steps

Aulet now teaches the 24 steps under six questions. This is the cleanest executive view:

1. **Who is your customer?** → steps 01–05, 07
2. **What can you do for them?** → steps 03, 06, 12–16
3. **How do they acquire the product?** → steps 05, 17, 24
4. **How do you make money?** → steps 08–11
5. **How do you design and build?** → steps 14–16, 19–20
6. **How do you scale?** → steps 18, 25–26

## What we refuse to import blindly

- **I-Corps “100 interviews”** as a gate for a solo Indian founder with a local services idea — too expensive. Keep the _spirit_ (talk to strangers, not friends).
- **VC TAM theatre** — 1% of India GDP is not a method.
- **“No business plans ever”** — banks, many Indian PSU tenders, and DPIIT/grant forms still want structure. Generate it from the pack when needed.
- **“Always SaaS subscription”** — India still runs on invoices, GST, and WhatsApp. Model follows customer, not fashion.
