# Step 07 — Competitive landscape and wedge

**Phase:** 2  
**Sources:** Aulet Steps 10–11 (core + competitive position); starter notes’ “why switch.” Lean Canvas unfair advantage.

## What this step is

Two pictures:

1. **Landscape:** named alternatives from step 03, plus who is funded, who owns the default, who is “good enough.”
2. **Wedge:** why a specific person would **switch** or **start paying**, on the two criteria they actually rank highest.

Aulet’s chart: X = persona priority #1, Y = priority #2. Plot you vs substitutes vs do-nothing. You need a story for the top-right, or you pick a different beachhead.

**Wedge examples that work:** a regulation (GST e-invoicing), a workflow lock-in (Tally), a geography (Kannada UI), a DMU (built for the CA, not the owner), a time-to-value (same-day setup vs 6-week ERP).

**Wedges that do not work:** “we have AI,” “better UX,” “we are cheaper” (without a cost core), “we are a platform.”

## Core (Aulet Step 10)

What will you be **uniquely** good at that is hard to copy in 12 months? Network effects, workflow data, distribution, a patent, a regulatory license, a two-sided density in one city. It is allowed to be **weak on day 0** — then you must say how it compounds. Unfair advantage on Lean Canvas may stay blank.

## Evidence required

- Named comparables with a fact each (price, feature, who they serve).
- Chart axes taken from the persona, not from the founder’s pride.
- A one-sentence switch reason a customer could repeat.
