# Step 06 — Market sizing (TAM / SAM / SOM)

**Phase:** 2  
**Sources:** Aulet Step 4 (beachhead TAM) and 14 (follow-on); investor custom of TAM/SAM/SOM. Starter notes asked for even rough $10M vs $10B.

## Definitions we will use (be explicit — people mix these)

| Term              | Meaning in our pack                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Beachhead TAM** | Annual **revenue you would earn at 100% share** of the narrow segment (Aulet). Bottom-up: # of end users × annual revenue per user. |
| **SAM**           | The part of that (or a slightly wider) market you can serve with your current offer, geography, and regulation.                     |
| **SOM**           | What you can realistically capture in 1–3 years given channels and budget. A plan, not a destiny.                                   |
| **Follow-on TAM** | Adjacent pins after you win the beachhead. Story for scale; **not** this year’s operating plan.                                     |

## Method (bottom-up first)

1. Count entities that match the beachhead definition (lists, associations, GST registrations in a cluster, school directories, housing societies — whatever is real).
2. Estimate annual spend per customer from **current alternatives** (step 03) and your intended price (step 09 may still be a draft — label `ASSUMPTION`).
3. TAM = count × ARPU.
4. Cross-check with a top-down report if one exists. If they disagree by 10×, you probably defined the segment wrong.
5. SAM/SOM: apply honest filters (language, city, who you can actually reach).

Aulet’s teaching range: beachhead TAM often **$20–100M/year** for a venture-scale IDE. Under ~$5M may still be a good **lifestyle or bootstrap** business — our product must not shame that. A $10B beachhead almost always means the founder said “India.”

## Evidence required

- Formula + sources + `ASSUMPTION:` tags (Harness G-d / G-h).
- Provenance: `founder_asserted` vs `externally_verified` (LaunchPad-v2).
- No “1% of a huge TAM” without a channel that could produce that 1%.

## Common failure modes

- Using global reports for a Pune niche.
- Counting users when you sell to companies (or vice versa).
- Mixing GMV with revenue for a marketplace.
